Rules of progression
  1. Double progression. Every lift has a rep range. Hit the top of the range on all sets → add the smallest increment next session and start again at the bottom.
  2. Log every set. Weight × reps. If it isn't written down you can't progress it.
  3. Stop 1–2 reps short on the first exercise of each session, closer to failure on the last two. In a deficit, recovery is the limit — not effort.
  4. Strength stalls around month 3. Normal in a cut. The goal becomes holding the weight while bodyweight falls. Same load at 95 kg bodyweight as at 103 kg is real progress.
  5. Deload weeks 13 and 22: same exercises, 60% of usual weight, stop well short. One week. It buys you the next six.
  6. Warm up 5 min on the bike plus 2 ramping sets on the first movement.

Don't waste money on: testosterone boosters · tribulus · fat burners · "male enhancement" pills · SARMs · MK-677 · large zinc doses. None of them will do a fraction of what losing 20 kg, sleeping properly and stopping smoking will do.

Worth saying plainly: this is general information, not medical advice. Before starting supplements — and especially before nicotine replacement — a quick conversation with your GP or pharmacist is worth the ten minutes.

Cardiovascular & sexual health

The honest version: the lifestyle changes are the intervention. Supplements are a rounding error next to them.

  • Stopping smoking is the single biggest lever here — it directly affects blood-vessel function, and improvement starts within weeks.
  • Losing the weight and the cardio work come next. Better cardiovascular health is better everything.
  • Sleep 7–9 hours. Under-slept and stressed undoes a lot of the above.
  • Foods worth eating often: eggs, sardines, olive oil, nuts, berries, bananas, spinach, tomatoes, broccoli, potatoes, oats, beans and lentils, lean meat — plus proper hydration.
  • If things haven't improved meaningfully by month 4 despite doing all of this, that's a conversation for a doctor, not a supplement shop.

Before the 25th: keep counting cigarettes and bring the number down, remove the easy ones, and break the routines that surround them — the coffee, the balcony, the drive. On quit day it's zero. If cravings are severe, ask a pharmacist or GP about patches, gum or lozenges rather than relying purely on willpower.

Hash stays at zero for the full six months. The point isn't moral — it's clarity, motivation, sleep, training consistency and self-control.

What to expect

Hash · days 1–14

Sleep is the big one — trouble falling asleep, vivid exhausting dreams for 1–2 weeks. Irritability, low appetite, night sweats, restlessness. Peaks days 2–6, meaningfully better by day 14, mostly settled across weeks 3–6.

Nicotine · days 1–10

Cravings peak days 3–5, and each individual craving lasts about 3–10 minutes — that's the entire fight. Irritability and poor concentration fade over 2–4 weeks. Physical dependence is largely gone by week 3; what's left is habit and trigger, which is a different problem with a different solution.

25 Aug – 1 Sep is the hardest week of the whole six months. Plan it now: nothing socially demanding, no deadlines you control, gym on every scheduled day, in bed early. Survive that week and the rest is arithmetic.
10-minute craving protocol

In order. Don't negotiate with the craving — negotiating is how you lose.

  1. Wait 10 minutes. That's the whole deal. You're not resisting forever, you're waiting out a wave.
  2. Drink a full glass of water.
  3. Leave the environment. Different room, outside, anywhere the trigger isn't.
  4. Walk 5–10 minutes.
  5. Chew gum.
  6. Ten slow breaths. This is your mindfulness practice doing actual work.
  7. Do something with your hands — dishes, tidy, anything.
  8. Reassess. It will almost always have passed. Log it in today's notes: what triggered it, what time, how strong.
If you slip: it's one day, not the end. Log it honestly, don't "restart Monday", don't starve yourself to compensate. Next meal and next session on plan. The people who finish are the ones who make slips small.
Morning routine
  1. 06:00 — wake. No phone. Not for anything.
  2. Water — a full glass before anything else.
  3. Bathroom, then weigh yourself and log it. Same conditions daily.
  4. Daylight — 10 minutes outside. Sets your body clock, which is what fixes the sleep the withdrawal is wrecking.
  5. 07:30 — train.
  6. Shower.
  7. 10 minutes of mindfulness, straight after the shower. Same chair, same time, every day. This is the anchor of the whole routine.
  8. Breakfast, then start the day.
Why after the shower. It's the strongest habit hook you have — the shower happens every single day whether you feel like it or not, so the sitting inherits its reliability. Don't move it. Towel off, sit down, ten minutes, then the day starts.
Mindfulness — how to actually do it

Sit upright in a chair, feet on the floor, timer for 10 minutes. Attention on the breath — count each out-breath to ten, then start again. You will lose the count constantly. Noticing that you lost it is the rep. That's the entire practice, and it's the exact skill that lets you catch a craving early enough to choose.

Guided is fine if silence is hard at first — Waking Up, Headspace, Insight Timer. After a month, try it unguided.

Evening, before bed: 5 minutes of the same, or a body scan. Then two lines written — one win, one thing that pulled at you, tomorrow's single priority.

Work & business
  • No phone in the first hour. Water, daylight, gym — before any screen.
  • Two × 90-minute deep work blocks, phone in another room, one named finishable task each. Not "work on the business".
  • Tomorrow's #1 priority written tonight. Decided calm, executed tired.
  • Admin and messages get a box — 14:30 to 15:30. Outside it, they wait.
  • Sunday business review, 30 min: what moved, what didn't, biggest lever next week, what to stop doing.
The clarity you get back in month two from being sober and sleeping properly is worth more to the business than anything you'd have shipped in month one. Be patient with the first four weeks.
